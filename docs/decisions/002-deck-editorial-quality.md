# Editorial depth and visual storytelling

Generating valid JSON, rendered pages and a searchable PDF does not establish the
quality of a learning deck. Real-provider acceptance must inspect both what the
reader learns and how the page communicates it. Synthetic provider fixtures test
contracts and failure handling; they are not visual quality examples.

## Evidence before presentation

Immutable source blocks retain their existing IDs and exact text. Adjacent blocks
are assembled into bounded evidence passages without crossing source, node or page
boundaries. Every character remains traceable to its original block span. This gives
authors enough context even when a PDF parser emitted many tiny text-operator
fragments, without rewriting the fact layer or invalidating previous citations.

Deck understanding uses a teaching dossier rather than a generic short abstract.
Hierarchical reductions retain specific mechanisms, distinctions, examples and
representative quotations; model context bounds remain explicit. Valid checkpoints
are reusable. Bibliography metadata and interpretations must not become invented
research findings. Caveats belong with the claim they qualify, with a concise
treatment of the overall evidence limits.
Quotations retain context and chronology: a recalled age at an earlier event is not
automatically an interview age. Translated quotations must be identified as such,
and paraphrases must not masquerade as literal quotes. Openings identify the work
when the actual evidence supplies it, and reserve detailed mechanisms for body pages.

## A model-authored storyboard

New plans add teaching points, a visual mode and an explanatory visual concept to
each page. The plan must advance distinct messages. It passes its visual intent to
semantic authoring and composition; adjacent page messages help avoid repetition.
Saved older plans remain readable. Illustration pages require an image request
during initial authoring; subsequent user revisions can change those requests.

There is no template inventory, fixed palette or mandated page sequence. The model
chooses an editorial visual world for the actual subject. Consistency comes from
typography, materials, palette and conceptual motifs; backgrounds, scale, lighting
and spatial organization can vary. Large text-free illustrations may form an
explanatory scene or a backdrop with planned calm reading areas. Readable words
continue to come exclusively from saved semantic content and deterministic rendering.
Image assets cannot become the source of claims, invented measurements or quotations.

Prompt/composition versions participate in reuse boundaries so completed output
from older design instructions is not mistaken for freshly composed output.

## Manual real-output acceptance

- Each explanatory page develops its message with source-specific detail, rather
  than repeating its title or a generic caution. The whole deck has an intentional
  progression and a useful synthesis.
- Major visuals explain relationships, processes, contrasts or a meaningful
  metaphor. Generated imagery is substantial where needed; diagrams communicate
  actual content, rather than repeating decorative nodes and cards.
- A recognizable visual language coexists with varied page composition and visual
  scale. All pages are inspected, including image cropping and text/background
  contrast. Ordinary explanations should normally remain readable at 32 px or more
  on the 1920×1080 canvas. The renderer now enforces that floor for main semantic
  text; font fitting is a safety net, not the design strategy. Actual image-background
  contrast is also checked before a new render is published.
- Every original citation resolves, every saved text fragment is searchable in the
  exported PDF, and exported page imagery agrees with the application previews.
- Scope, checkpoint recovery, single-page editing and unchanged-page reuse retain
  their existing tests. Subjective quality is documented by inspected real outputs,
  not by assertions about synthetic mock pages.

User reference PDFs and private real-provider outputs stay outside Git. Benchmark
artwork may be inspected locally; the application produces new subject-specific
illustrations rather than embedding or copying that artwork.
